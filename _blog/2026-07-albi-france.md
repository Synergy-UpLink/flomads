Albi is a city in southern France and the prefecture of the Tarn department, in the Occitanie region. It sits on the Tarn River about 85 kilometers northeast of Toulouse and has roughly 50,000 residents.

## A bit of history

Albi gave its name to the Albigensians, the medieval Cathar movement that was suppressed in the Albigensian Crusade. In the aftermath, the bishops of Albi built in a style meant to project power. The Palais de la Berbie, a fortified bishop's palace, was completed by the end of the 13th century, and construction of the Cathedral of Sainte-Cécile began in 1282. A trade in woad, a plant used to make blue dye, brought later prosperity.

## What it's known for

- **Cathédrale Sainte-Cécile.** Often described as the largest brick cathedral in the world, with an interior painted from floor to ceiling.
- **The Cité Épiscopale.** The cathedral, palace, and surrounding old town were listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2010.
- **Toulouse-Lautrec.** The painter Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec was born here in 1864, and the Berbie palace now houses the world's largest public collection of his work.
- **The Pont Vieux.** A bridge over the Tarn that dates to the 11th century and is still in use.

## What it's like

Almost everything is the same warm orange-red brick, which gives the whole town a glow in the evening light.
