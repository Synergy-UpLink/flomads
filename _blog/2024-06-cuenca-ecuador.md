Cuenca (officially Santa Ana de los Cuatro Ríos de Cuenca) is a city in the Andean highlands of southern Ecuador and the capital of Azuay Province. It sits at roughly 2,500 meters (about 8,200 feet) above sea level, has well over 300,000 residents, and is Ecuador's third-largest city after Guayaquil and Quito.

## A bit of history

The Spanish founded the city in 1557 on the site of an earlier Inca settlement, Tomebamba, itself built over a Cañari city. Its historic center was added to the UNESCO World Heritage list in 1999. The "four rivers" in its name are the Tomebamba, Yanuncay, Tarqui, and Machángara.

## What it's known for

- **The New Cathedral.** The blue and white domes of the Catedral de la Inmaculada Concepción dominate the skyline. Construction began in the 1880s and ran for most of a century.
- **Panama hats.** Despite the name, they are woven from toquilla straw in Ecuador, and Cuenca has long been a center of the trade.
- **Cajas National Park.** A highland landscape of lakes and páramo, an easy trip west of the city.
- **Walkability.** Cobbled streets, riverside paths, and markets.

## Climate

Spring-like temperatures year-round, cool nights, and frequent afternoon rain.
