Limoges is a city in central France, on the Vienne River, and the capital of the Haute-Vienne department in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region. It has roughly 130,000 residents.

## A bit of history

The Romans founded a town here called Augustoritum. In the Middle Ages Limoges became known for its enamel work, and in 1768 deposits of kaolin (the white clay at the heart of true porcelain) were discovered nearby at Saint-Yrieix. Porcelain workshops soon followed, and the name Limoges has been attached to fine china ever since.

## What it's known for

- **Porcelain.** Still made in and around the city, with several factories and museums to show for it.
- **Cathédrale Saint-Étienne.** A Gothic cathedral begun in 1273 whose construction stretched over several centuries.
- **Gare de Limoges-Bénédictins.** A 1929 railway station topped with a tall clock tower, often named among the most beautiful stations in France.
- **The old bridges.** The medieval Pont Saint-Étienne still crosses the Vienne.

## What it's like

Smaller and quieter than it looks on a map, and very walkable. It is a city that rewards slowing down.
