Romford is a town in the London Borough of Havering, on the eastern edge of Greater London. It was historically part of Essex and became part of Greater London in 1965.

## A bit of history

The name comes from Old English words meaning "the wide or spacious ford," a crossing on the main road from London to Colchester. The River Rom was actually named later, from the town, not the other way around. Romford has been a market town since 1247, when a royal order established a weekly Wednesday market, and the market is still going today, which makes it older than most things in London.

## What it's known for

- **The market.** Open several days a week in the town center.
- **Transport.** Romford is about 12 miles from central London and is served by the Elizabeth line, which runs straight through the center of the city.
- **A suburban hub.** Shopping, nightlife, and a large catchment of the east London and south Essex suburbs.

## What it's like

Busy and unglamorous in the best way, where Essex meets London. It is a good place to see how the capital fades into the countryside at its edges.
