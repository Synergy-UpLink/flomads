Hà Nội (Hanoi) is the capital of Vietnam, in the north of the country on the Red River delta. More than 8 million people live in the city, which makes it the country's second largest after Ho Chi Minh City.

## A bit of history

The city dates to 1010, when Emperor Lý Thái Tổ moved the royal capital here and named it Thăng Long, "Ascending Dragon." It became Hà Nội, roughly "inside the rivers," in 1831. The French made it the capital of French Indochina in 1902, and the colonial architecture they left behind (wide boulevards, the Opera House, rows of shuttered villas) still shapes the center. Hà Nội became the capital of reunified Vietnam in 1976.

## What it's known for

- **The Old Quarter.** A tangle of narrow streets that grew up around trade guilds. Many street names still begin with *Hàng* ("merchandise") followed by whatever used to be sold there.
- **Lakes.** Hoàn Kiếm Lake sits in the middle of the city and gets its name from a legend about a magic sword returned to a golden turtle. West Lake (Hồ Tây) to the north is much larger.
- **The Temple of Literature.** Founded in the 11th century and often called Vietnam's first university.
- **Food.** Phở, bún chả, and egg coffee (*cà phê trứng*), which was invented here in the 1940s.
- **Motorbikes.** Millions of them.

## Climate

Hà Nội has four distinct seasons: hot, humid summers and cool, damp winters, with a pleasant stretch in the autumn in between.
